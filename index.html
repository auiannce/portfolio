About Me

Hi, I'm Auiannce Euwing, a Computer Science graduate from Carleton College with a passion for cybersecurity, data analytics, and software development. I enjoy solving complex technical problems and transforming data into meaningful insights that help organizations make informed decisions.

My experience spans information security, data science, IT support, and research. I've worked with technologies such as Python, Java, SQL, Splunk, Jamf, Nessus, Palo Alto firewalls, and Linux while supporting enterprise systems, analyzing security events, building data visualizations, and developing analytical tools. Whether I'm investigating security incidents, creating dashboards, or writing code, I enjoy learning new technologies and tackling challenging problems.

I'm particularly interested in cybersecurity operations, cloud security, data analytics, and automation. I value writing clean, maintainable code, collaborating with diverse teams, and continuously expanding my technical knowledge.

Outside of technology, I enjoy exploring new ideas, contributing to meaningful projects, and finding creative solutions to difficult problems. I'm always excited to connect with others who share a passion for technology and innovation.

Posters & Paper

Algorithms in Real Life: Where Theory Meets the Real World

Poster + teaching materials connecting core CS algorithms like Merge Sort and Binary Search to real-world stakes in algorithmic decision-making, including how scoring systems like COMPAS and decile scores can reflect or reinforce bias.

  • View Poster (PDF)

Maximizing Player Satisfaction: Two-Player Gnomes Game

Research poster on synthesizing collaborative and equilibrium strategies in a two-player, semi-cooperative game. This work models stakeholder preferences over outcomes and uses a Pareto-style approach to balance trade-offs between players.

  • View Poster (PDF)

Paper: Maximizing Player Satisfaction (TACC / REU)

Companion paper to the Gnomes Game poster. It develops an algorithm for equilibrium strategies in two-player semi-cooperative games with possibly incomplete preferences over temporal goals, using a Pareto accumulation approach to find mutually agreeable strategies.
